Send, land, follow up

A finished memo is not a delivered decision. It still has to reach the right inbox in the right order, be read before the meeting or rescued in it, and leave a record that says what was decided on which version. Part 1 · covers the cover email and the timing

The cover note and when it goes out

5 min live

The cover email is the first thing the reader sees, before the memo, so it follows the memo's own rule: bottom line first. The decision and the date go in the subject line. The body is two or three sentences, says what is attached, and says who has already seen it. Then the timing: early enough to be read, late enough to still be current.

LiveThree sentences and a subject line3 min▶

The cover email is the smallest executive document there is, and it gets the same treatment as the memo. The subject line carries the decision and the date: "Decision needed 14 November: shared customer record (memo v3 attached)". A subject line of "Customer data memo" makes Marguerite open the email to find out whether it matters; one that states the decision answers that from the inbox.

The body is two or three sentences. The first repeats the ask in one line. The second says what is attached or linked, and which version. The third says who has already seen it: "Kenji has seen it and supports it, and Halvard has checked the numbers." That last sentence does more work than it looks. It tells Marguerite that the two questions she would ask first have already been asked. The one-line recap of the principle: conclusion first, support after, as taught in Communication, Structure ↗.

Self-studyWho sees it before the reader does3 min▶

Kenji Arai is the sponsor, and the one rule about a sponsor is that they are never surprised. He sees the memo before anyone else, early enough that his comments can still change it. Halvard Lind checks the numbers next, because Marguerite will ask him before she signs; a cost table he has already corrected is a cost table he will defend. Only then does the pre-read go to Marguerite, and the cover email can truthfully say both have seen it.

The order of those conversations, and how to hold them so nobody learns about the proposal from a meeting invite, is stakeholder work, taught in Stakeholder Management, Managing up ↗ and Coalitions and pre-wiring ↗. Here we only make sure the page and the email reflect what those conversations settled.

Self-studyWhen the pre-read should land3 min▶

There is no measured best day. The common practice is a trade-off: send too early and the memo is read once, forgotten, and overtaken by events; send the night before and it is not read at all. A few working days before the meeting is the usual answer, adjusted for the reader's calendar. If Marguerite travels the two days before 14 November, the pre-read goes before she leaves.

The memo feeds one slot in a steering or executive meeting whose rhythm someone else owns. How those forums run, and the cadence of updates around them, is taught in Data PMO, Stakeholders and steering ↗ and Data PMO b6 ↗. Ask whoever owns the agenda when papers are due, and send yours before that.

Real world

No rulebook, a shared habit. None of the sources behind these pages prescribes a cover email or a send date; the subject-line rule, the "who has seen it" sentence and the few-working-days window are common practice in executive and staff writing, assembled here as one routine. Treat them as defaults to adjust to your reader, not as findings.

Part 2 · covers what to do when it is not read

When the memo is not read

5 min live

Plan for the morning the pre-read was not read, because it will come. The meeting starts, Marguerite says she has not got to it, and you have three good moves and one bad one. The good moves keep the decision tied to the page. The bad one throws the page away and starts presenting.

LiveThree moves, in order of preference4 min▶

A silent read in the room. If the slot has time, ask for the first few minutes to read the page together, in silence, then discuss. A one-page memo is short enough that this costs the meeting very little, and everyone then argues from the same text.

The first sentence, said out loud. If there is no time to read, say the memo's first sentence as your two-line summary: "I recommend one shared customer record for sales, the depots and billing, at €1.4 million over two years, and I ask you to approve it today." If you wrote the first sentence well in session 1, this is the summary; you do not need another.

Move the decision. If the decision cannot responsibly be taken on one sentence, and a €1.4 million spend may well be one of those, ask to move it to the next slot rather than have it decided unread. A decision taken on a summary nobody checked is the one that gets reopened in three months.

Self-studyWhy not turn it into slides on the spot3 min▶

The tempting fourth move is to present the memo: walk through it section by section, or open a deck you made "just in case". It feels helpful and it undoes the work. The page put the decision on top; a presentation puts it at the end of the talk, after the background, which is the draft order session 1 rewrote. It also starts a second version of the argument that nobody has checked against the memo Halvard signed off.

Decks have their own craft and their own place, taught in Visual Storytelling ↗. In a decision meeting about a memo, the memo is the document.

Real world

Amazon reads in silence first. Jeff Bezos's 2017 letter to shareholders, published in April 2018, describes Amazon's meetings opening with the narrative memos read silently by everyone, "a kind of study hall" (read at source). The letter says Amazon does not do slide presentations; the often-repeated story that Amazon "banned PowerPoint" by an email in 2004 is reported, not read here. Amazon's memos run to six pages; a one-page memo makes the same move far cheaper.

Part 3 · covers versions and the decision record

Versions, and the record afterwards

5 min live

Two small habits save a large argument later. A version line on the memo, so every comment and every decision maps to a text. And a short decision record after the meeting, so what was decided, by whom and on what conditions is written down once, by the person who wrote the memo, while everyone still remembers.

LiveThe version line2 min▶

One line at the top of the memo, in small type under the title: the version number, the date, and what changed since the last version. "v3 · [date] · changed since v2: cost table checked by Halvard." It costs ten seconds and prevents the worst conversation in a decision meeting, the one where Halvard is reading a cost table that no longer exists.

The rule is simple: every copy that leaves your hands carries a number, and the number goes up every time the text changes. When Halvard sends comments, they are comments on v2; when the pre-read goes, it is v3; when Marguerite decides, she decides on v3, and the record says so.

LiveThe decision record3 min▶

Within a day of the meeting, Sunniva writes a short note to everyone who needs to act on it: what was decided, by whom, on which version of the memo, the conditions attached, and the date it will be reviewed. At Fernhollow (constructed) the obvious condition is the one from the risk block: Rafe's joint owner is named before the build starts. The stop signal from session 5 travels into the record too, so the people running the build know what would halt it.

In the language of session 1, the record is an information memo: it changes what people know and asks nothing. It goes on top of the file, not inside a thread, so that in three months "what did we actually agree?" has one answer.

Self-studyWhen the answer was not yes3 min▶

Write the record whatever the outcome. "Deferred to the next meeting, pending Halvard's view of the year-two cost" is a decision too, and writing it down turns a vague "not yet" into a named condition and a date. "Refused" is worth recording with its reason, because the same proposal, or a better one, will come back, and the person who brings it should know what it has to answer.

A record that disagrees with someone's memory is better found the next day than at the review. Send it to the people in the room and ask them to say if it is wrong; silence after a reasonable time is the closest thing to agreement you will get.

Real world

Say what would change your mind. The US intelligence community's analytic standards, ICD 203, ask analysts to express and explain the uncertainties behind their major judgments, including the indicators that would change them (Office of the Director of National Intelligence, 2015 as amended, standard (2), read at source). A stop signal is that idea applied to a plan, and carrying it into the decision record is what makes someone watch for it.

Build-along

Assemble Sunniva's final memo

★ 22 min · everyone builds

Every piece of Sunniva's page has been written across five sessions. Now it goes together, section by section, as one page, with the cover email and the decision record template beside it. The worked text is the constructed Fernhollow Freight example, the same rewrite the session 4 bench loads; build your own memo next to it, one section at a time.

The top: title, version line and the decision. The title from session 1, the version line from tonight, then the bold first sentence carrying the decision, the ask, the deadline and the cost, with two short sentences of support under it.

★ Step 1 · the top of the pageDecision needed by 14 November: approve the shared customer record v3 · [date] · changed since v2: cost table checked by Halvard The decision I recommend one shared customer record for sales, the depots and billing, at €1.4 million over two years, and I ask you to approve it at the executive meeting on 14 November. (bold) That date lets work start in January and the first depot go live in Q3. Kenji supports the proposal and his team would run the platform inside the existing infrastructure budget.

Why now. Two short paragraphs. The first sentence of each carries the point; the numbers are the constructed memo facts, one per claim, as in session 3.

★ Step 2 · why nowWhy now Our three customer lists disagree on name, address or contact for more than half of our 2,140 active accounts. Sales, the depots and billing each keep their own, and nothing links them. The disagreement already costs us money we can count. About 310 credit notes last quarter were for deliveries to an address the customer had already corrected, and five of the six account managers we asked keep a private spreadsheet because they do not trust the CRM.

What it costs, and what it returns. The four-line cost table, then the return you can count and the one you cannot yet. Saying that retention is not measured costs nothing with Halvard and buys a great deal.

★ Step 3 · cost and returnWhat it costs, and what it returns | Item | Year 1 | Year 2 | | Platform and build | €900,000 | €200,000 | | Depot and sales time | €180,000 | €120,000 | The return we can count today is fewer credit notes and less re-keying in the depots. The larger return, retention on key accounts, is plausible but not yet measured, so it is not in the case.

What could go wrong. The risk block from session 5: the main risk as the first sentence, what is already being done, the stop signal, then the second risk.

★ Step 4 · the risk blockWhat could go wrong The main risk is that the CRM account lists are not opened up early, so the platform launches empty. Rafe has agreed to name a joint owner from his team before any build starts. A second risk is depot staff time during the cut-over, which is why the rollout moves one depot at a time and stops if the credit-note rate does not fall.

The options we rejected, and what happens next. Each rejected option with its real advantage before the reason it lost. Then the next steps if the answer is yes, and where the detail lives.

★ Step 5 · options and next stepsThe options we rejected Cleaning the three lists once is cheapest, but on our measurements they would drift back within a year. Making the CRM the master would suit sales and leave the depots entering data into a system built for account management. What happens next If you approve on 14 November, the joint owners are named in the first week of December and the build starts in January. The detailed plan and the matching method are in the appendix for anyone who wants them.

The cover email. Decision and date in the subject line; three sentences; who has already seen it. Send it after Kenji and Halvard, never before.

The one-page memo (v3) is attached. Kenji has seen it and supports it, and Halvard has checked the numbers. Sunniva

The decision record template. Write it now, with the blanks, so on the day of the meeting it takes five minutes. The conditions and the stop signal are already known; only the outcome and the review date are not.

★ Step 7 · the decision recordDecision record: shared customer record Decided: ____ (approved / approved with conditions / deferred / refused) By: Marguerite Vance, executive meeting, 14 November On: memo v3 Conditions: Rafe's joint owner named before the build starts Stop signal: the rollout stops if the wrong-address credit-note rate does not fall at the first depot Review date: ____ Sent to: ____ (everyone who acts on it)

★ Step 8 · your memo on the benchSunniva's rewrite: 5 of 5 read by 30 s, the ask at 10.8 s Your memo: ____ of 5 read by 30 s the ask at ____ s words before the ask: ____ If a must-survive element is late, move it up, not out.

One page, then the routine. The page is the memo, the email is how it travels, the record is how it ends. Keep the three templates side by side; the next memo starts from them, not from a blank page.

Homework

Before your next memo goes out

30 min
  1. Assemble your one page. Every section from steps 1 to 5 for your own memo, with a version line, and run it on the session 4 bench once more.
  2. Plan the order. Write down who sees it before the reader does, in order, and the date each conversation happens. The pre-read goes only after the last one.
  3. Draft the cover email. Subject line with the decision and the date, three sentences, who has seen it. Read the subject line alone: could the reader act on it from the inbox?
  4. Fill the record template. Everything you can know before the meeting: the version, the conditions, the stop signal, who it goes to. Leave only the outcome and the review date blank.
Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and session 6 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · What should the subject line of Sunniva's cover email say?

The subject line is the first line the reader sees, so it carries the decision and the date, and naming the version ties it to a text. A names a topic, C says it matters without saying why, and D will not fit an inbox line.

2 · The meeting starts and Marguerite says she has not read the pre-read. The slot has time. What is the best move?

A silent read keeps everyone on the same text and costs little for one page; Amazon opens its meetings this way (Bezos 2017 letter, read at source). B starts a second, unchecked version of the argument; C asks for a decision on a page nobody read; D takes longer than reading it.

3 · Which of these must a decision record contain?

The record answers "what did we agree?" in three months' time: the version shows what was approved, and the conditions and review date, such as Rafe's joint owner and the stop signal, say what has to stay true. A repeats the memo, B loses the conditions, C is minutes, not a record.
