Learn Executive Briefings with Phoebe

One page, the decision first, the evidence behind it: how to write for someone with four minutes. Six sessions for data and analytics leads, and the practitioners who write up for them, on the decision memo, the pre-read and the briefing as artifacts. Who reads it and what goes on top, which format wins when, the evidence that earns its place, how to show options, risk and confidence honestly, and how to get the page read and the decision recorded. One constructed running case, a logistics company's head of data asking her CEO to approve a customer-data platform, and one bench: a skim-survival test computed in your browser on four versions of her memo, or on yours.

The six sessions 🧭 · from the reader to the decision record

The running case is Fernhollow Freight, a constructed mid-size logistics company, continued from Stakeholder Management with the same people. Sunniva Holm, head of data, has mapped who decides; now she writes the one-page memo asking Marguerite Vance, the CEO, to approve one shared customer record across sales, the depots and billing. Fernhollow and everyone in it are invented for teaching, and every page says so.

📝 Session 1 · foundations

Who reads it, and the decision on top

The reader with four minutes, decision memo against information memo, a title that states the decision, and a reader card built from the stakeholder map.

▶ Start here 🗂️ Session 2 · core

Pick the format

One-page decision memo, pre-read, Amazon's narrative memo, a bottom-line-first note and Churchill's 1940 minute on brevity, read at source where possible: when each one wins.

the right shape 📎 Session 3 · core

The evidence behind it

One number per claim with its source line, the one exhibit that earns its place, the appendix as the place detail goes to be optional, and what to cut.

one number per claim ⏱️ Session 4 · bench night

The skim-survival bench

Four versions of one memo, or your own text, read by a stated skim model. Which of the decision, ask, deadline, cost and risk survive 30 seconds, 90 seconds and four minutes.

★ the bench ⚖️ Session 5 · deeper

Options, risk and confidence language

The recommendation with the rejected options shown fairly, the risk you name before they do, and one confidence sentence per claim, from Sherman Kent's 1964 essay to the US intelligence standard.

say how sure 📬 Session 6 · deeper

Send, land, follow up

The cover email, when a pre-read goes out, what to do when it is not read, versions, and the decision record afterwards. Capstone: Sunniva's final one-page memo assembled.
