# Official course map - learn-executive-briefings-with-phoebe

Executive Briefings & Memos. Bucket comm, single-track, 6 sessions of 45 minutes, session 4 = the
computed bench. Audience: data and analytics leads, and the practitioners who write up for them,
writing ONE page to an executive who has four minutes. Built 2026-11-08 by Phoebe Fu.

Card blurb: "One page, the decision first, the evidence behind it: how to write for someone with
four minutes."

## Evidence tiers

- **read at source**: the primary text itself was read on 2026-11-08; what was read is named.
- **catalogue read**: an archive's catalogue record was read (reference, date, title), not the image.
- **abstract read**: the abstract or index record was read; anything beyond it is not claimed.
- **reported**: only secondary sources were read. Pages say "reported" or "is often cited as".
- **practitioner model**: a book or house framework with no peer-reviewed test found. Pages say so.
- **constructed**: invented for teaching (Fernhollow Freight). No number about it is a measurement.

## Running case (constructed, label "Constructed Fernhollow Freight example" every time)

Fernhollow Freight continues from learn-stakeholder-management-with-phoebe, same people, same roles.
Sunniva Holm, head of data (reports to Kenji Arai, CIO), wants one shared customer record across sales,
the depots and billing approved. The stakeholder course made **Marguerite Vance, CEO, the decider: she
signs** anything above the capital threshold, and she asks Halvard Lind (CFO) and Odile Mercer (COO)
before she signs. So the memo is written TO Marguerite; Halvard is the reader who checks the numbers
and Kenji is the sponsor who must never be surprised. Rafe Okoro (commercial director) owns the CRM
account lists; Bram Tully (depot planning) is asked first on anything touching the depots.

Constructed memo facts (identical in all four bench versions; none is a measurement): 2,140 accounts
ordered in the last year; the three lists agree on name, address and contact for fewer than half;
about 310 credit notes last quarter for wrong-address deliveries; five of six account managers keep
a private spreadsheet; €1.4 million over two years (platform and build €900,000 + €200,000; depot and
sales time €180,000 + €120,000); executive meeting 14 November; build starts January; first depot
live in Q3; main risk: the CRM account lists not opened up early, so the platform launches empty;
second risk: depot staff time during the cut-over.

## Sessions (titles grepped against every sibling h1 and title on 2026-11-08: no collisions)

### 1 Who reads it, and the decision on top (`01-the-decision-on-top.html`)
- The reader has minutes, not an afternoon; decision memo (asks for a yes or no) vs information memo
  (changes what the reader knows, asks nothing). The title states the decision.
- The reader profile from the stakeholder course is the input: Marguerite decides; Halvard checks.
- Figure: the four minutes as a timeline of what the reader actually looks at (the bench's skim order).
- Build-along: write the one-line decision, the title, and a reader card for your own memo.
- Health line (landing + s1): writing pressure treated as workload and planning, never medical advice.

### 2 Pick the format (`02-pick-the-format.html`)
- Decision memo, pre-read, narrative long memo, BLUF note; when each wins.
- Amazon narrative memos: Bezos's 2017 letter to shareholders, published April 2018 (SEC exhibit 99.1,
  read at source): "We don't do PowerPoint (or any other slide-oriented) presentations at Amazon";
  "narratively structured six-page memos"; silently read at the start of each meeting in "a kind of
  study hall"; a great memo "probably should take a week or more". Pages quote at most one short line.
- The 2004 email banning slides in S-team meetings: reported, not read.
- BLUF: US Army AR 25-50 (2020 edition, Preparing and Managing Correspondence). The official PDF could
  not be fetched (armypubs host unreachable, mirrors 403). The bottom-line-first rule is REPORTED via
  secondary Army training material that cites AR 25-50 (recommendation, conclusion or reason for
  writing in the first or second paragraph). A Fort Jackson regulation FJ REG 1-1 (1 April 2010,
  read at source) says staff papers place "the bottom line up front" and are used with AR 25-50.
  Recap only; link Communication 03.
- Churchill, "Brevity", memorandum to the War Cabinet, 9 August 1940, The National Archives CAB 67/8/11,
  former reference WP (G) (40) 211 (catalogue read). Content REPORTED from a published transcription:
  papers far too long; main points in "a series of short, crisp paragraphs"; complicated factors or
  statistics to an appendix; often an aide-memoire of headings only; drop padding phrases.
- Figure: decision tree of formats.

### 3 The evidence behind it (`03-the-evidence-behind-it.html`)
- One number per claim, with its source line; the exhibit that earns its place (one chart or table);
  the appendix as the place detail goes to be optional (Churchill's appendix point, reported);
  what to cut. Constructed Fernhollow numbers only.
- ICD 203 standard (3) distinguish underlying information from assumptions and judgments (read at
  source) used as the source-line discipline.

### 4 The skim-survival bench (`04-the-skim-survival-bench.html`) - canon below
- Brysbaert (2019), J. Memory and Language 109:104047, abstract read at source: 190 studies, 18,573
  participants; adults' silent reading of English: 238 wpm non-fiction, 260 wpm fiction; most adults
  175-300 wpm for non-fiction; oral reading 183 wpm; the estimates are lower than numbers often cited.
- Nielsen Norman Group, "F-Shaped Pattern of Reading on Web Content" (2017 update of 2006 eyetracking
  work, read at source): the F-pattern is the default when no strong cues attract the eye, and is
  "bad for users and businesses"; other patterns named (layer-cake: headings; spotted; marking;
  bypassing; commitment); advice: front-load headings, bold important words. Teach the disagreement:
  this is web-page eyetracking from a practitioner firm, not a study of executives reading memos;
  generalising it to memos is an assumption.
- The skim multiplier is an ASSUMPTION; the fixed reading order is a simplification.

### 5 Options, risk and confidence language (`05-options-risk-and-confidence.html`)
- Recommendation plus the rejected options shown fairly; the risk you name before they do.
- Sherman Kent, "Words of Estimative Probability" (CIA, Studies in Intelligence; date 1964 reported,
  text read at source in the CIA release approved 22 Sept 93): NIE 29-51 (March 1951) called an attack
  on Yugoslavia "a serious possibility"; Kent meant about 65 to 35 in favour; the Policy Planning
  Staff read much lower odds; Board members' readings ran from about 20 to 80 to 80 to 20. His chart:
  certainty 100; almost certain 93 give or take about 6; probable 75 give or take about 12; chances
  about even 50 give or take about 10; probably not 30 give or take about 10; almost certainly not 7
  give or take about 5; impossibility 0.
- ODNI ICD 203 Analytic Standards (signed 2 January 2015, as amended; read at source): likelihood
  terms with ranges: almost no chance / remote 01-05%; very unlikely / highly improbable 05-20%;
  unlikely / improbable 20-45%; roughly even chance / roughly even odds 45-55%; likely / probable
  55-80%; very likely / highly probable 80-95%; almost certain / nearly certain 95-99%. Products must
  not combine a confidence level and a degree of likelihood in the same sentence. Standard (2):
  properly expresses and explains uncertainties. Standard (3): distinguish information from
  assumptions and judgments.
- Link Decision Intelligence 06 for the trust evidence; never re-derive it.

### 6 Send, land, follow up (`06-send-land-follow-up.html`)
- The cover email, timing of a pre-read, what to do when it is not read, versioning, the decision
  record afterwards. All common practice (no single source); Amazon's silent reading at the start of
  the meeting (Bezos letter, read at source) as one answer to "not read".
- Hand-off: the board pre-read belongs to the planned Presenting to a Board course.
- Capstone: Sunniva's final one-page memo assembled.

## Bench canon (session 4) - computed 2026-11-08, python == node on 1,223 values

Model: parse (title = first line; # headings; paragraphs and sentences; **bold**; - / * / 1. list
items; | table rows). Skim order: title, headings, first sentence of each paragraph, bold runs in
later sentences, list items and table rows, remaining sentences. Rate 238 wpm x M (default M = 1.5,
357 wpm, assumption; slider 1.00 to 2.00 in 0.25 steps). An element is read when the unit holding it
is finished. Checkpoints 30 s, 90 s, 4 min (240 s). Keyword rules: decision = recommend(s),
recommendation, decision, decide; ask = I ask, we ask, ask you, asking you, please, I need you, need
your, I request, we request; deadline = deadline, by Monday..Friday, a day number plus month; cost = a
£ $ € sign before a digit; risk = risk(s). Tags [decision] [ask] [deadline] [cost] [risk] override.
Words before the ask = words in a straight top-to-bottom read before the first ask unit.

Default M = 1.5 (the widget prints reach times to one decimal):

| Version | words | words before ask | read by 30 s / 90 s / 4 min | ask at |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Draft, work-it-out order | 449 | 427 | 0 / 5 / 5 | 75.5 s |
| Rewrite, decision on top (BLUF) | 402 | 12 | 5 / 5 / 5 | 10.8 s |
| Over-long, everything in | 1651 | 1635 | 2 / 4 / 4 (risk at 266.1 s) | 44.0 s |
| Buried ask | 336 | 242 | 2 / 5 / 5 | 48.2 s |

Anti-lever "add context up front" (92 words: a 48-word opening sentence, then two more):
- Draft: 0/5/5 -> 0/2/5 (90 s loses decision, ask, deadline); ask 75.5 -> 90.9 s; words before ask 427 -> 519.
- BLUF: 5/5/5 -> 4/5/5 (30 s loses risk, now 32.8 s); ask 10.8 -> 18.8 s; words before ask 12 -> 104.
- Over-long: 2/4/4 -> 1/4/4 (30 s loses cost, now 35.0 s); ask 44.0 -> 52.1 s; 1635 -> 1727.
- Buried: 2/5/5 -> 2/5/5 (no checkpoint changes); ask 48.2 -> 63.7 s; 242 -> 334.
Honest sizing: the anti-lever ALWAYS makes the ask later and adds words before it; whether it crosses a
checkpoint depends on how close the memo already sat. At M = 2.0 it changes no checkpoint on the BLUF.

Break button "bury the ask":
- BLUF: 5/5/5 -> 3/5/5 (30 s loses ask and cost); ask 10.8 -> 53.8 s; words before ask 12 -> 217.
- Over-long: 2/4/4 -> 2/2/4 (90 s loses ask and deadline); ask 44.0 -> 113.9 s; 1635 -> 511.
- Buried: unchanged 2/5/5; ask 48.2 -> 51.3 s (it was already in paragraph 4).
- Draft: ask 75.5 -> 60.0 s, words before ask 427 -> 308: the draft's ask was in the last paragraph,
  so moving it into paragraph 4 brings it FORWARD. The break hurts a memo that had its ask on top.

Other multipliers (quote only with the multiplier named): M = 1.0: BLUF 4/5/5 (risk 37.1 s), draft
0/0/5, over-long 1/4/4. M = 2.0: over-long 2/4/5 (risk at 199.5 s).

No real public decision memo with a clear reuse licence was added in this build: the four versions
are constructed, and the widget and pages say so. Candidate for a later build: a UK ministerial
submission published under the Open Government Licence.
